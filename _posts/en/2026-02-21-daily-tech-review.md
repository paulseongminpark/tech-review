## Today's Key Summary
NVIDIA expanded its AI infrastructure dominance through a major multiyear partnership with Meta, deploying millions of Blackwell and Rubin GPUs alongside first large-scale Grace CPUs and future Vera CPU-only servers, signaling a potential CPU supercycle in AI data centers. Benchmarks like SemiAnalysis' InferenceX v2 highlighted NVIDIA GB300's superior 100x performance over H100 in inference tasks, while AMD's MI355X competes in FP8 but trails in composability. China's rapid power capacity expansion, six times faster than the US, bolsters its AI race position amid global hardware shortages, with Western Digital sold out of drives through 2026. CES 2026 showcased chip advancements from Intel, AMD, and NVIDIA, underscoring relentless innovation in AI-optimized processors and systems.

## Major Announcements & Products

### NVIDIA-Meta AI Infrastructure Expansion
NVIDIA and Meta announced a multiyear partnership on February 17, 2026, for on-premises, cloud, and AI infrastructure, including millions of Blackwell and Rubin GPUs, Spectrum-X Ethernet networking, and Confidential Computing for WhatsApp. Notably, Meta commits to immediate large-scale deployment of standalone Grace CPUs, with Vera CPU-only servers planned for 2027, marking a shift toward CPU-heavy AI workloads. This deal optimizes Meta's infrastructure for advanced AI training and inference, potentially igniting demand for NVIDIA's Arm-based CPUs in data centers.

### SemiAnalysis InferenceX v2 Benchmark
SemiAnalysis released InferenceX v2 on February 16, 2026, an open-source suite benchmarking AI inference across leading hardware. It reveals NVIDIA's GB300 NVL72 delivers up to 100x better performance than H100, while AMD's MI355X excels in FP8 but lags in FP4 composability. The benchmark aids developers in evaluating real-world inference efficiency for frontier models on next-gen chips.

### Western Digital HDD Sellout for 2026
Western Digital reported all hard drives sold out for 2026 due to surging AI data center demand, with long-term agreements extending into 2027-2028. This shortage underscores infrastructure bottlenecks as hyperscalers stockpile storage for massive AI datasets. Production ramps cannot yet match the explosive growth in exabyte-scale needs.

## Business Strategy & Partnerships

### xAI Acquired by SpaceX
SpaceX announced the acquisition of xAI to form a vertically integrated "innovation engine," deploying one million solar-powered orbital data centers to bypass terrestrial power limits. This bold strategy leverages SpaceX's launch capabilities for space-based AI compute. It positions the combined entity to lead in unconstrained AI scaling.

### NVIDIA Teases World-Surprising Chips at GTC 2026
NVIDIA CEO Jensen Huang announced major processors to "surprise the world" at GTC 2026 (March 16-19), likely Rubin GPUs with HBM4 following talks with SK Hynix. Focus remains on AI data center hardware, not consumer GPUs. This reinforces NVIDIA's lead amid intensifying competition.

### OpenAI Enters AI Hardware with Smart Speaker [unverified]
Reports indicate OpenAI assembled a 200-person team for AI-powered devices, starting with a $200-300 camera-equipped smart speaker slated for 2027 release. Aiming to compete with Meta's glasses and others in physical AI interfaces. OpenAI has not confirmed, keeping plans speculative.

## Trends & Insights

### CPU Supercycle in AI Infrastructure
NVIDIA's Meta deal pioneers standalone Grace and Vera CPU deployments, hinting at a CPU supercycle as AI shifts toward diverse compute mixes beyond GPUs. This complements GPU millions while addressing inference and efficiency needs in massive clusters. Expect broader adoption as Arm-based CPUs prove viable for hyperscale AI.

### China Powers Ahead in AI Race
New data shows China adding power capacity six times faster than the US, enabling massive AI data center builds despite US restrictions. This infrastructure edge supports domestic chip and model scaling. Global competition intensifies as energy becomes the new bottleneck.

### Hardware Shortages Grip Data Centers
Western Digital's 2026 HDD sellout and chip benchmark races highlight supply constraints amid AI boom, from storage to power. CES 2026 previews like Rubin AI chips and Ryzen AI Max+ signal innovation, but production lags demand. Hyperscalers secure multi-year deals to front-run shortages.
