## 1. NVIDIA-Meta Deploy Millions of Blackwell and Rubin GPUs with First Large-Scale Grace CPUs

NVIDIA and Meta announced a multiyear partnership on February 17, 2026, covering on-premises, cloud, and AI infrastructure.

**Why it matters:** Meta committing to immediate large-scale deployment of standalone Grace CPUs — with Vera CPU-only servers planned for 2027 — signals that AI workloads are shifting from GPU-centric to diverse compute mixes, potentially igniting a CPU supercycle in AI data centers.

- Millions of Blackwell and Rubin GPUs, Spectrum-X Ethernet networking, and Confidential Computing for WhatsApp included
- Meta commits to immediate large-scale standalone Grace CPU deployment; Vera CPU-only servers planned for 2027
- Deal optimizes Meta's infrastructure for advanced AI training, inference, and private AI features

**What's next:** NVIDIA's Arm-based CPUs will transition from GPU supplements to standalone workload processors in hyperscale AI data centers.

## 2. SemiAnalysis InferenceX v2 Shows NVIDIA GB300 Delivers 100x H100 Inference Performance

SemiAnalysis released InferenceX v2 on February 16, 2026 — an open-source suite benchmarking AI inference across leading hardware platforms.

**Why it matters:** An open-source, real-workload-based benchmark independent of vendor marketing provides credible decision-making data for infrastructure investment — and a 100x performance gap over H100 is the kind of number that drives upgrade cycles.

- NVIDIA GB300 NVL72 delivers up to 100x better performance than H100 in inference tasks
- AMD MI355X excels in FP8 but lags in FP4 composability
- Benchmark aids developers in evaluating real-world inference efficiency for frontier models on next-gen chips

**What's next:** Independent benchmarks like InferenceX will become the standard reference for data center chip procurement decisions, reducing vendor-controlled narrative influence.

## 3. Western Digital Sold Out of Hard Drives for All of 2026, Long-Term Agreements Extend to 2028

Western Digital reported all 2026 hard drives sold out due to surging AI data center demand, with long-term agreements extending into 2027–2028.

**Why it matters:** HDD — not GPU — emerging as an AI infrastructure bottleneck demonstrates that the AI boom is stressing the entire infrastructure stack beyond compute, reaching storage and power in ways that cannot be quickly resolved.

- All 2026 hard drives sold out due to surging AI data center demand
- Long-term agreements already extending into 2027–2028
- Production ramps cannot match the explosive growth in exabyte-scale storage needs

**What's next:** Persistent HDD shortages will accelerate hyperscaler adoption of alternative high-density storage options like QLC NAND to fill the gap.
