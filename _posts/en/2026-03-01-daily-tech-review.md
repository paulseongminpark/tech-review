## 1. Frontier Model Competition Breaks Through Performance Stagnation in One Month

Google's Gemini 3.1 Pro was released on February 19, achieving 77.1% on the ARC-AGI-2 benchmark. This is more than double the performance of the same series' Gemini 3 Pro, putting pressure on Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6 (68.8%) and OpenAI's GPT-5.3-Codex just three weeks prior.

**Why it matters:** It is overcoming limits in measuring pure reasoning ability that seemed impossible. Unlike 2024, when technical indicators appeared stagnant, the fact that Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and DeepSeek released next-generation models almost simultaneously in February 2026 signifies an extreme intensification of the competitive landscape. Benchmark competition has intensified to the point where each lab questions, "Is this really a necessary improvement?"

- Gemini 3.1 Pro achieved 94.3% on GPQA Diamond (PhD-level science questions), surpassing Claude Opus 4.6's 91.3%. At the same time, its cost is lower at $2/$12 per million tokens compared to competing models.(https://deepmind.google/models/model-cards/gemini-3-1-pro/)
- Claude Opus 4.6 specializes in agentic coding capabilities, topping Terminal-Bench 2.0. Its 1M token context window and agent team features are tailored for enterprise deployment scenarios.(https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/blog/claude-opus-4-6-anthropics-powerful-model-for-coding-agents-and-enterprise-workflows-is-now-available-in-microsoft-foundry-on-azure/)
- DeepSeek V4 (scheduled for release in February) was announced with 1M+ token context and a 1 trillion parameter architecture, focusing on optimization for Chinese chips (Huawei) to reduce reliance on U.S. chips.(https://pandaily.com/deep-seek-to-release-v4-multimodal-model-next-week-with-native-image-video-and-text-generation-support)

**What's next:** Model performance competition is likely to shift from benchmark "score wars" to competition over real production deployment reliability.

## 2. "2028 AI Mass Unemployment" Shakes Markets, But Reality Accelerates Investment

On February 22, Citrini Research's viral essay "The 2028 Global Intelligence Crisis" presented a white-collar unemployment scenario due to AI (S&P 500 -38%, unemployment rate 10.2%). This caused the Dow to drop 1.7% on Monday, February 24, and drove the IGV Software Index to a year-to-date decline of -27%. However, reality moved in the opposite direction that same week.

**Why it matters:** While investor sentiment was shaken by "bubble fears," actual capital allocation by megacap tech companies accelerated instead. This reveals the gap between simple investor emotions and corporate decision-making. Additionally, institutions like Citadel Securities quickly issued rebuttal papers, and real labor market data (11% YoY increase in software engineer demand) contradicts the Citrini hypothesis, showing how exaggerated fear scenarios are rapidly mistaken for reality.

- Amazon announced a $50B investment commitment to OpenAI in February (initial $15B, followed by $35B), designating AWS as the exclusive cloud deployment partner for OpenAI Frontier. This signals intensifying leadership battles between Microsoft and Amazon in an era where annual AI investment is projected to rise from $37B in 2025 to $660B in 2026 (combined for the top 4 megacaps).(https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/aws/amazon-open-ai-strategic-partnership-investment)
- Meta disclosed $162-169B in AI infrastructure spending for 2026 and signed a multi-generation partnership with AMD (6GW of Instinct GPUs). It simultaneously maintained a multi-generation contract with Nvidia, adopting a portfolio approach.
- Nvidia's Q4 FY2026 (ended January 25) revenue was $68.1B, up 73% year-over-year, with datacenter revenue alone at $62.3B (75% YoY), hitting an all-time high. The Q1 FY2027 guidance announced on February 26 predicts $78B, an additional 14.7% quarterly growth.(https://www.marketbeat.com/originals/nvidias-ai-boom-isnt-slowing-after-blowout-q4/)

**What's next:** As AI investment solidifies from "optional" to "essential asset allocation," industry capital concentration will intensify further through 2027.

## 3. Regulation of AI Datacenter Power Crisis — White House Rate Payer Protection Pledge

U.S. electricity rates rose 6% year-over-year, with AI datacenter demand cited as a major cause. President Trump declared in his February 24 State of the Union address that he would impose self-power generation obligations on big tech companies, and on February 26, White House spokesperson Taylor Rodgers announced that Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, xAI, Oracle, and OpenAI are scheduled to formally sign the "Rate Payer Protection Pledge" at the White House on March 4.

**Why it matters:** This means AI infrastructure expansion has escalated from a mere technical issue to a national energy policy matter. Moreover, despite companies already making voluntary commitments in January-February, the administration forcing a formal signing ceremony is an attempt to institutionalize "unreliable voluntarism." At the same time, for this measure to have real effect, a regulatory framework is needed to determine "which datacenters are responsible for electricity price hikes," and the absence of that is the core problem.(https://www.foxnews.com/politics/scoop-trump-brings-big-tech-white-house-curb-power-costs-amid-ai-boom)

- Microsoft (January 11), OpenAI (January 26), Anthropic (February 11), and Google (battery project on February 26) all announced they would bear their own energy costs. However, Arizona Democratic Senator Mark Kelly countered that "handshake agreements are insufficient; legal guarantees are needed."(https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/25/the-white-house-wants-ai-companies-to-cover-rate-hikes-most-have-already-said-they-would/)
- Google's battery project announced for its Minnesota datacenter was called the "world's largest scale," but specific size or local environmental impact assessments were not disclosed. This reveals the opacity of corporate-led energy transitions.(https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/25/the-white-house-wants-ai-companies-to-cover-rate-hikes-most-have-already-said-they-would/)
- Datacenter self-power generation is expected to add new stress to natural gas, turbine, solar, and battery supply chains. Energy supply chain bottlenecks are likely to soon become bottlenecks for AI expansion.(https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/25/the-white-house-wants-ai-companies-to-cover-rate-hikes-most-have-already-said-they-would/)

**What's next:** Following the March White House signing ceremony, each company will disclose details of their actual power generation plans, which will fundamentally alter AI infrastructure ROI calculations for 2026.

**The real flow of the AI/Tech industry from February 23-28 cycles as "intensification of performance competition (frontier model wars)" → "solidification of investment scale (ignoring bubble fears)" → "institutionalization of infrastructure constraints (energy)."**
