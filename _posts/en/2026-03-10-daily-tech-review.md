## 1. Meta Begins Turning AI Capital Expenditures into Profits, Ascending to Top Spot in Magnificent 7

Meta presented evidence that its aggressive $115-135 billion AI infrastructure investment planned for 2026 is translating into actual advertising revenue growth, reversing investor concerns. In 2024 ad revenue, an estimated over 1 trillion KRW in added value was generated from AI-based recommendation engines and improved ad targeting, confirmed by a 5% increase in Facebook usage time and a 10% surge in Threads.

**Why it matters:** Whether big tech companies' trillions of dollars in AI capex will convert into actual revenue is a key question for the 2026 market, and Meta has clearly demonstrated this, restoring investor confidence. This serves as a signal strengthening the justification for AI investments by competitors like Microsoft, Google, and Amazon.

- Meta's family of apps (Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Threads) is maintaining mid-to-upper tier revenue growth rates, while pursuing both API revenue and direct commercialization through a dual strategy of open-source Llama models and closed-source Avocado models.
- Through the multi-year Meta-NVIDIA partnership (announced March 8), it is building ultra-large clusters based on Vera Rubin and SpectrumX Ethernet, while parallel investments in Google TPUs have achieved chip supply diversification.
- Despite capital cost pressures, Meta's clear free cash flow and ROI path have heightened the likelihood of a profitability turnaround by 2026.

**What's next:** Meta's AI investment returns are expected to begin reflecting in concrete financial figures starting from Q2 2026.

## 2. Iran-Israel War Hits Amazon Data Centers, Exposing Physical Vulnerabilities of Cloud Infrastructure

Iran's drone retaliatory attacks directly struck Amazon AWS data centers in the UAE and Bahrain, demonstrating that commercial cloud infrastructure has become a new military target in conflict zones. Amazon's announcement on March 2 of the strike incident led to outages for numerous services including Middle East financial firms, payment companies, and delivery apps.

**Why it matters:** At a time when global big tech firms were concentrating data center investments in the Middle East for cheap energy and land availability, the realization of physical attack threats is expected to sharply increase infrastructure security costs and region-specific risk premiums. This will significantly raise data center construction time and costs.

- The AWS Abu Dhabi facility required at least 24 hours for damage recovery, with dual damage occurring to generators and cooling systems.
- Underground bunker-style data centers cost over $2,000 per square foot—twice that of surface facilities—and take over 3 years, posing issues, so security enhancements for existing above-ground data centers (blast-resistant windows, AI surveillance drones, automated patrol robots) are being selected as the next best option.
- Two Tehran data centers were also simultaneously targeted, suggesting that data centers must be re-evaluated as defense targets on the level of national strategic infrastructure.

**What's next:** Governments in the US, Europe, and the Middle East are expected to introduce regulatory policies applying national-level critical infrastructure protection standards to commercial data centers.

**Source:** [Data centers are caught in the crossfire of the Iran war](https://www.semafor.com/article/03/03/2026/data-centers-are-caught-in-the-crossfire-of-the-iran-war)

## 3. Apple Releases 7 New Products Weekly to Seize AI PC Market and Restructure Price Tiers

Apple unveiled en masse on March 2-4: MacBook Neo (A18 Pro, $599), iPhone 17e (A19, MagSafe, $599), M5 MacBook Air/Pro, M4 iPad Air, and two Studio Display models, injecting price leadership into the AI device competition in the PC market. The MacBook Air doubled base storage to 512GB, and with neural processing accelerators embedded in each GPU core, AI tasks are 9.5 times faster than M1.

**Why it matters:** Amid intensifying big tech AI chip supply competition driving up DRAM prices and overall PC costs, Apple has strategized to expand its education and new user base by launching entry-level Macs at Chromebook-competitive prices. This creates upgrade paths to higher-end models, strengthening ecosystem lock-in.

- The MacBook Neo's A18 Pro uses the same chip as the iPhone 16 Pro (2024), with fixed 8GB RAM and single external display limit to minimize costs, enabling the $599 price point.
- The iPhone 17e features the C1X modem (2x faster 5G than iPhone 16e), added MagSafe, doubled base storage to 256GB, and with iOS 26 and Apple Intelligence, enables on-device AI execution.
- The launch of the high-end Studio Display XDR ($[unconfirmed]) completes Apple's vertical price portfolio from the $599 budget tier to premium creators market.

**What's next:** Sales figures and new user ratios to be released after April will verify whether Apple's pricing strategy effectively boosts PC market share and ecosystem expansion.
