## Today's Key Summary

The second and third weeks of February (2/10–2/18) can be summarized across four axes. Agentic AI has evolved beyond a single model into multi-agent orchestration systems, beginning to establish itself as the foundational infrastructure for enterprise workflows. Big Tech's four companies are pouring $650 billion in CAPEX into AI infrastructure, elevating the AI investment S-curve to the level of a national-scale energy project. Physical AI and neuromorphic vision chips have raised robot and smart infrastructure response speeds to above-human levels, and the resumption of large-scale M&A in AI and security has signaled a full-fledged industry restructuring.

## Major Announcements & Products

### OpenAI Frontier — Enterprise Agent Platform
OpenAI Frontier is a platform that allows companies to onboard AI agents like new employees, assigning roles and permissions for management, with support for 1M token context and 128k output. Integrated with CRM, data warehouses, and HR semantic layers, agents have entered the "digital colleague" stage of directly manipulating real business systems. Permission management and audit logs are core design elements, and the role of enterprise security teams is expected to expand significantly.

### Anthropic Claude Opus 4.6
Claude Opus 4.6 supports parallel subtask delegation through agent teams functionality, providing 1M context and 128k output. It recorded 65.4% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 agentic coding and an Elo rating of 1,606 on GDPval-AA, leading agentic coding benchmarks. The fact that it officially supports parallel multi-agent design positions it as the reference model for building large-scale automation pipelines.

### Google Gemini Deep Think & GLM-5
Google Gemini Deep Think is a specialized reasoning mode designed for solving advanced research problems in mathematics, physics, and computer science—specifically targeting hard problems in science and engineering. GLM-5 is an open-source model with 754B parameters under an MIT license, distributed via Hugging Face and ModelScope, and optimized for agentic tasks and long-horizon engineering. Both models are eroding the conventional wisdom that "open-source lags behind by a generation."

### Neuromorphic Vision Chip
Beihang University and the Beijing Aerospace Research Institute announced a neuromorphic vision chip that mimics the lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN) structure of the human brain. Robot motion recognition speed improved 4× over humans, latency was reduced by 75%, and recognition accuracy improved by 100%. Enabling real-time object tracking and collision avoidance in autonomous robots and smart manufacturing lines, this technology significantly accelerates the practical deployment of Physical AI.

## Business Strategy & Partnerships

### Big Tech $650B CAPEX
Amazon's ~$200B, Alphabet's $175–185B, Meta's $115–135B, and Microsoft's ~$105B combine for a total of $650 billion in Big Tech AI infrastructure investment for 2026. This is a 67–74% increase over the previous year, with 75% concentrated in AI chips, servers, and data centers—elevating AI infrastructure to a national-scale energy and power project. With surging power demand and growing green AI requirements arising simultaneously, carbon footprints are likely to become a regulatory or market requirement.

### The Great Rebound — M&A Resurgence
The $21.4B Devon-Coterra merger, the conclusion of Alphabet's $30B Wiz acquisition, and a $25B PANW-CyberArk proposal are among the large deals in AI and security that have surged, re-igniting the M&A market. The pent-up demand for consolidation from the high-rate period is rapidly emerging, aligned with AI-driven growth opportunities.

### OpenAI — $40B Partner Investment Negotiations
OpenAI is negotiating $40 billion in partner investments with Nvidia (up to $20B), Amazon, and Microsoft. As a vertically integrated alliance structure of AI model–cloud–chip forms, vendor lock-in risk for specific cloud or chip vendors is becoming a central variable in enterprise AI strategy.

### AAIF (Agentic AI Foundation) Launch
The Agentic AI Foundation, with participation from OpenAI, Anthropic, Block, Microsoft, AWS, and Cloudflare, was launched under the Linux Foundation. Centering on AGENTS.md and MCP, it pursues agent interoperability, self-verification, and memory standardization, representing a move to capture the common infrastructure specification of the agent ecosystem.

## Trends & Insights

### Standardization and Governance of Agentic AI
As agent interoperability standards (AGENTS.md and MCP) begin to settle under the Linux Foundation, individual company agent implementations are expected to converge toward a portable structure built on a common layer. Since agents hold actual system permissions, self-verification, memory management, and rollback frameworks have emerged as the key breakthrough for AI adoption in 2026.

### Physical AI and Edge AI Decision-Making Proliferation
Physical AI ranks as the #1 tech trend of 2026, with neuromorphic chip-based robots demonstrably exceeding human reaction speeds. As edge AI decision-making becomes standard across autonomous robots, smart manufacturing, and self-driving infrastructure, the transition from cloud-dependent AI architecture to edge-cloud hybrid design is expected to accelerate.

### Labor Market and the Productivity Paradox
According to TEKsystems research, companies report experiencing productivity gains from AI while simultaneously rescheduling digital transformation timelines due to increasing system complexity. As San Francisco Fed President Daly's "AI Moment" speech implies, a consensus is forming that it takes an adaptation period for AI's productivity benefits to distribute across the broader labor market, and that policy responses must accompany the transition.
