## 1. Meta goes dual-vendor: 6GW AMD deal + NVIDIA partnership simultaneously — hyperscalers hit $63B

Meta announced a multi-year contract with AMD for up to 6GW of Instinct GPUs (estimated $6B potential revenue) on February 24, while simultaneously finalizing a multi-generation partnership with NVIDIA covering millions of Grace CPUs, Blackwell and Rubin GPUs. Combined AI infrastructure spending by Amazon, Google, Meta, and Microsoft hits $63B in 2026, up 62% from $38.8B in 2025.

**Why it matters:** Contracting both AMD and NVIDIA simultaneously is a vendor diversification strategy, not just procurement. Meta is co-developing chips and ROCm software with AMD to reduce long-term dependency on the NVIDIA ecosystem. The $63B figure rivals the historic scale of the late 1990s telecom investment cycle.

- AMD Helios rack architecture with MI450 custom GPUs ships in Q2 2026; AMD receives 160M performance-based warrants from Meta
- NVIDIA keeps Meta as its second-largest customer, stating "no one deploys AI at Meta's scale anywhere"
- Amazon allocates $5B for government AI infrastructure alone (adding 1.3GW across AWS Top Secret/GovCloud)

**What's next:** AMD MI450 shipment performance in late 2026 will be the first real test of whether AMD can materially challenge NVIDIA's data center dominance.

## 2. DRAM prices surge 90% — AI demand shock hits smartphones and auto industry

DRAM prices jumped 90% in Q1 2026 versus Q4 2025. AI data center demand for high-bandwidth memory is absorbing the majority of output from the big three — Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron (93% of global supply) — structurally squeezing consumer and automotive supply. Micron announced it will fully exit the consumer Crucial brand to focus on AI server customers.

**Why it matters:** Unlike the COVID chip shortage, this is a structural shift driven by AI, not a transient demand spike. Memory fab expansions require tens of billions and years to build — Intel's CEO publicly acknowledged "no relief until 2028." Consumer DDR5 32GB kits jumped from $100 to $350; global smartphone shipments are forecast to fall 13% from 1.26B units in 2025 to 1.11B in 2026.

- Automotive DRAM prices expected up 70-100% in 2026; Apple is paying double for LPDDR5X from Samsung for iPhone 17
- IDC: "Tariffs and the pandemic look like jokes compared to this crisis"
- Shortage expected through 2028 as AI HBM demand continues cannibalizing standard DRAM production lines

**What's next:** Additional fab investment announcements from Samsung and Micron expected in H2 2026; the timing of peak DRAM prices will become a critical variable in AI investment ROI calculations.

**Source:** [S&P Global - DRAM Shortage Automotive Impact](https://www.spglobal.com/automotive-insights/en/blogs/2026/02/what-auto-marketers-and-dealers-need-to-know-about-the-dram-shortage)

## 3. NVIDIA Rubin, TSMC 2nm, China split — three forces reshaping the semiconductor ecosystem

NVIDIA unveiled the Rubin platform targeting up to 10x lower inference token cost versus Blackwell and 4x more efficient MoE training. TSMC raised its dividend 28% and guided for 100K 2nm wafers/month in 2026. Meanwhile, NVIDIA still hasn't booked revenue from China despite receiving Trump administration approval to sell H200 there.

**Why it matters:** Three forces are simultaneously redesigning the AI infrastructure ecosystem. If Rubin cuts inference unit costs dramatically, the adoption threshold for AI services drops and demand could re-explode. TSMC 2nm wafers exceeding $30K each (2x 4nm) means AI chip cost structures face sustained upward pressure. The China split is accelerating the risk of a bifurcated global AI ecosystem.

- Rubin platform: Vera CPU + Rubin GPU + NVLink 6 + ConnectX-9; AWS, Google, Azure, OCI first to deploy in Q2 2026
- TSMC Q1 2026 revenue guidance +38% YoY; forecasts AI accelerator CAGR of 50%+ through 2029
- DeepSeek V4 optimized for Huawei chips first, no pre-access given to NVIDIA/AMD — semiconductor geopolitical split accelerating

**What's next:** Once Rubin-based products ship in Q2 2026 and real inference cost reductions are published, a price competition among AI service providers is expected to begin in earnest.
